​service design + discovery research for clinical trial design​

methods: service design, product design, UX design, research

tags: pharma, stigma, medical design, clinical trials, patient experience, empathy

outputs: app designs, clinical trial recommendations

As a healthcare researcher and designer, I have led many patient and caregiver experience projects with pharmaceutical, biomedical, and health tech clients. Here is a case study showing some sanitized insights from two related projects on clinical trials.

This is a combination of two projects, one for a client on protocol for a cancer trial and one for a client on shift worker diary entries in clinical trials. *Note: This is real client work. To preserve confidentiality, I scrubbed identifiers and had ChatGPT generate a fake name, Averion. These slides show how I can turn research insights into impactful reports and clear design recommendations for clinical trials.

goals

Study A: The team wanted to understand patient perceptions of clinical trial recruitment and participation. They wanted feedback on trial flyers and protocol. They wanted to understand how and where patients sought out information and what were support needs specific to XX cancer patients.

Study B: The team noticed issues with shift workers completing diary entries (”compliance”) in clinical trials, and wanted to understand how to design trials to accommodate them.

methodology

Each project was planned slightly differently with phases depending on various goals.

iterative design & research process

Participants were shown 3 different flyer examples and asked to mark them up, then explain answers. One flyer design was picked and iterated on, then shown to focus group participants to discuss. Participants unanimously approved.

Participant support needs informed trial design recommendations.

Confusion and stigma showed the need for education and marketing redesign.

Participant suggestions for clinical trial diary entries wereused to design a new diary entry system.

insights embedded in design artefacts

PAIN POINT! confusion, stigma

Clinical trials often seen as “last resort.” They need to be normalized in public health.

Recommendations: Embed flyers and information in community events, hospital advocacy groups. Make materials accessible to both patients and caretakers since patients during treatment are often homebound.

Led to FLYER REDESIGN

PAIN POINT! placebo mistrust

Some patients did not want to participate in trials if placebos were a possibility, seeing it as a “waste of time.”

Recommendations: Educate patients on possible benefits: even those in the placebo group will receive standard of care and other support, still making it a worthwhile option.

Led to EDUCATIONAL CAMPAIGN DESIGN

PAIN POINT! lack of support

Patients felt like a lack of support, for themselves and for their caretakers, was a major barrier to clinical trial participation.

Recommendations: In addition to regular support (logistics, travel, financial), there are other specific needs that were mapped out in an entire support ecosystem.

Led to CLINICAL TRIAL EXPERIENCE REDESIGN

example design based on insights

Shift workers said that reminders, larger time entry windows, the ability to go back and edit entries, and gamified incentives (!) would help with completion rate. While the first three seemed straightforward conclusions, the gamification was a new spark. We hadn't thought that in such a serious study, gamification was of interest. We thought they wanted something simple and sterile because it was a task to 'get over with.' The idea that fun badges and challenges were not only welcome but also sought after changed the design direction of the app.

Here are some mockups I created on Figma that showcase notifications and gamification.

impact

The teams were able to better understand complexity + full ecosystem of support needs. They changed clinical trial protocol, recruitment information, experience, and design. They were interested in follow-up work and sharing insights across the organization.

learnings and takeaways

The projects were research projects that turned into design projects. More stakeholder input during the process would better inform the details of the service blueprint and pinpoint what changes could be made and what teams could be involved. (eg teams could not change certain things about trial sites). To create a full blueprint, a workshop with these stakeholders could be conducted.

There is potential around redesigning the service of distributing clinical trial information + redesigning the experience of a clinical trial: both would require more cross-functional buy-in.
