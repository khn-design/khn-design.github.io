How to read the I Ching by Khanh Nguyen

The I Ching is an ancient Chinese oracle based off yin and yang. There are eight trigrams: earth, mountain, water, wind, thunder, fire, lake, heaven. These stack to create the 64 hexagrams. Hexagrams are built from the ground up.

To call on a hexagram, you can use three coins. Toss the coins six times to build the hexagram. Heads are 3 points. Tails are 2 points. 6 points is old yin, a changing line. 7 points is young yang, a non-changing line. 8 points is young yin, a non-changing line. 9 points is old yang, a changing line.

A changing line shifts from yin to yang or yang to yin. That means you can have two hexagrams: the original and another where any changing lines have shifted states.

For example, one of my first readings! The tosses were: 7, non-changing yang; 9, changing yang; 9, changing yang; 8, non-changing yin; 8, non-changing yin; 8, non-changing yin. This built hexagram 11, grand, which then became hexagram 24, return.

I would read these two judgements and pay most attention to the second and third changing lines, seeing what resonates.

Those are the basics! Happy reading!

THE PROCESS

Curious about my zine-making process? I started out with paper sketches to get the gist of the story I wanted to tell.

In my final zine, there were five elements: the front cover; the first spread which explained the background of the I Ching, trigrams, and hexagrams; the second spread which explained the coin toss method and changing versus non-changing lines; the third spread which gave an example of how to build a hexagram in a reading; and the back cover.

As you can see, in my first prototype, I was still trying to figure out spacing. I also had a spread that explained in more detail how hexagrams were built, but realized the information wasn't as necessary, and that it made the example more cramped. In the next version, I wanted to give the example its own spread, so I condensed the information so that I could get to the coin toss spread earlier.

This second prototype had the basic structure of the story I wanted to tell. I added some more design elements, such as the logo design that is on the front of all of my I Ching cards in the deck I am designing. Now I was ready to digitize it.

I drew each panel on Procreate and then added the text on Illustrator. Then I laid out the panels on Illustrator so they would print out correctly, in the right orientation after I folded them. This was where the paper prototype helped, as I could see what I needed to lay out where. I then printed the page in color, then fold it into the zines.
